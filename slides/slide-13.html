The bytes you send aren't the bytes they get.

DNS poisoning, a CDN or subdomain takeover swaps your stylesheet in transit — and browser extensions and ad-blocker filter lists inject their own CSS on top, on every page the victim loads.

Problem: you control what you ship, not what arrives.

Evil CSS will reach your page. The only question left is what it can do once it's there.

Beat 4 defeat and the pivot. Even frozen, self-hosted CSS isn't safe end to end: it can be swapped in transit (DNS poisoning, CDN/subdomain takeover) or added to by the environment (extensions, ad-blocker filter lists inject CSS on every site). Land the teal line hard — it closes the delivery ladder and hands off to the next ladder: you can't keep it out, so what happens once it's in? Straight into "render some user HTML, how hard can it be?" ~45 seconds.
