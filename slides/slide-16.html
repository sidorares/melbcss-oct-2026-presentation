No data leaves. The attacker just repaints your trusted UI.

FAKE INPUT FIELDS

-webkit-text-security turns a plain box into a password field. Forge a login, a payment form, a system prompt.

HIDDEN WARNINGS

display:none or an overlay buries the real “external sender” banner, the fraud alert, the “are you sure?”.

HIJACKED CLICKS

:before/:after inherit an element's click. “Move to spam” quietly does something else — CSS hotwiring.

SPOOFED UI

A position:fixed gadget escapes the message box and repaints the whole app. PortSwigger defaced Outlook and spoofed its login — pure CSS.

No exfiltration. Just a user doing the wrong thing because the page lied to them.

Fills the "direct visual manipulation" gap — the most intuitive CSS attack for this room, and the one with no data theft at all. Four techniques: (1) fake fields via -webkit-text-security and restyling; (2) hiding real security warnings/banners with display:none or an overlay; (3) click hijacking where :before/:after pseudo-elements inherit the target's click handler (PortSwigger's "CSS hotwiring" — every click performs an action you didn't intend); (4) full UI spoofing/defacement via a position:fixed "CSS gadget" that breaks out of the email message window and repaints the app (PortSwigger defaced Outlook and spoofed its login screen with sanitized CSS). The point to land: the damage here is the user being deceived into acting, not data leaving. ~60 seconds. Then into "fine, strip the scripts."
